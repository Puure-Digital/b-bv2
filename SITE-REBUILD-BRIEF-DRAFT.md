# Beams & Braces — Site Rebuild Brief (Draft for Review)

This is a first pass at capturing everything discussed, so we can read through it together, adjust anything that's off, then fold it into one final document to use as a design brief and build blueprint — something we could hand to a designer/developer and have them build the site from it without needing to ask us follow-up questions.

Nothing here is final. Flag anything that's wrong, missing, or overstated.

---

## 1. The core directive

Don't let this read as AI-generated. That means:

- **No generic AI-design defaults** — no warm-cream-and-terracotta template, no near-black-with-one-neon-accent template, no centered hero with a gradient blob, no `rounded-lg` everywhere, no stock "safe" font pairing (Inter + Inter, or Inter + a generic serif).
- **Unique typography is the priority lever.** If the current fonts (Fraunces + Plus Jakarta Sans) still feel too close to a common AI default once we see it fully built out, we source and license/download a font pairing that's genuinely distinctive to this business — something with real character that a template generator wouldn't reach for. This might mean a custom or lesser-used display face paired with a clean, functional body face.
- **Every design decision should trace back to something true about Beams & Braces** — its materials, its craft, Darren's actual history — not a generic "premium outdoor brand" mood board.

## 3. Message and tone of voice

*Draft — needs your input to firm up:*

- Written in first person as Darren where it counts (About/history), consultative rather than sales-y elsewhere.
- Direct, plain-spoken, expert — not corporate hype, not generic "transform your outdoor space today!" marketing copy.
- Confident about craft and standards (real materials, real build methods) without sounding boastful.
- **Open question for you:** how formal or casual should this be? Closer to a tradesperson talking to a homeowner over a fence, or closer to a premium lifestyle brand's copywriting? Some middle ground between those two is probably right, but worth pinning down before writing a full page of copy in the wrong register.

## 4. Design continuity

- One visual system applied identically across every page — same type scale, same spacing rhythm, same colour usage rules, same component patterns (buttons, cards, section dividers) — so no page reads as if it was built in a different sitting.
- This was already flagged as unfinished on the current build (some pages more polished than others) — the rebuild should treat this as a hard requirement from page one rather than something to reconcile afterwards.

## 5. What the site needs to cover

### 5.1 Beams & Braces as a business
- Who they are: a premium composite decking and outdoor living specialist based in Norwich, trading name of D Gooch Limited.
- What makes them different from a generic local decking fitter: TrexPRO Platinum installer status, a seat on Trex's own Pro Advisory Council, and a genuine specialism in curved decking (among the UK's leading builders of it).
- Service area: Norfolk and Suffolk, with a natural emphasis on the Norfolk Broads, waterside properties, and holiday homes.

### 5.2 History
- Family roots in timber/joinery going back to 2002 (Darren's own earliest memory, age three, in the family yard).
- Beams & Braces / composite decking specifically begins 2016.
- TrexPRO Platinum status achieved 2017.
- Invited onto Trex's Pro Advisory Council in 2024.
- 500+ composite decks installed since 2016, including some of the UK's largest curved decks.
- This should read as a real narrative arc (a timeline, a story), not a bullet list of disconnected facts.

### 5.3 What Darren's expertise actually gets the customer
- Not just "years of experience" as a vague claim — the specific, tangible things that experience translates into:
  - Real build standards other installers skip (UC4-rated joists, joist protection tape, plastic subframes on every job).
  - The ability to do genuinely difficult work well (curved decks, complex waterside/heritage sites) that most installers wouldn't attempt.
  - A direct relationship with Trex itself (feeding back what's actually working on real UK job sites, not just fitting off a spec sheet).
  - The outcome: decks built to still be exactly right in twenty years, not just decks that look good in the first photo.

### 5.4 The "luxury outdoor living" positioning
- Reframe the product from "composite decking" (a material category) to "the outdoor space you've been picturing" (a lifestyle outcome).
- Decking, lighting, and railing/trellis work together as one outdoor living package, not three separate services bolted together.
- Should connect emotionally to how people actually use the space: entertaining, relaxing, extending the home outward — not just technical durability claims.

### 5.5 Product detail — needs full, real depth on each

**Trex decking ranges** (verified real colours/warranties, already corrected on the current build — see `BRAND-GUIDELINES.md` for the exact table):
- Signature — Ocracoke, Whidbey — 50-year warranty
- Transcend — Jasper, Biscayne, Rainier, Carmel, Island Mist — 50-year warranty
- Enhance Naturals — Foggy Wharf, Rocky Harbor, Toasted Sand, Calm Water — 25-year warranty
- Enhance Basics — Clam Shell, Tide Pool — 25-year warranty

Each range needs real depth: what it's made from, why someone would choose it over another range, real board photography (not approximated colour swatches), and the genuine warranty/durability facts.

**Lighting** — currently thin on the live site, needs real expansion: what lighting options are offered, how it integrates with the decking, real project photography, not just a passing mention.

**Trellis / railing** — same treatment as lighting: currently under-developed, needs its own proper depth rather than being an afterthought bolted onto the decking pages.
